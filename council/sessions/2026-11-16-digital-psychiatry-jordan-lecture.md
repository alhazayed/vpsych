# Council session — 2026-11-16 — Digital Psychiatry Jordan Lecture

Source: `Digital_Psychiatry_Jordan_15_Minute_Lecture.pptx` (12 slides). The user asked the council to judge the lecture itself as a professional bet, not a product.

## Flat paragraph

A 15-minute, 12-slide lecture that Dr. Alaa Zayed, a Jordanian psychiatrist who also runs an AI-simulated-patient training platform for therapists (Vtraining, an Arabic/English product), plans to give to the Jordanian Psychiatrists Committee. Its argument: digital psychiatry (telepsychiatry, clinical AI, digital assessment such as PHQ-9/GAD-7, digital therapeutics and VR, wearables, electronic records and e-prescribing) improves access but creates a dependency risk — system outages, cyberattacks on sensitive psychiatric data, AI error and cultural bias, erosion of clinical skills through automation bias, digital exclusion of patients, and vendor lock-in. It says AI can draft notes and support differentials but diagnosis, suicide/violence risk and treatment decisions must stay with the clinician. It forecasts five shifts over 5–10 years (AI-assisted workflow, continuous monitoring, personalized care, immersive therapies, Arabic-first access). It uses a recent US air-traffic-control disruption as a resilience analogy (the slide itself still carries a note saying the FAA figures are preliminary and must be verified before delivery), then a hypothetical 24-hour national digital healthcare outage in Jordan, and four clinical vignettes (a schizophrenia follow-up missed, bipolar medication history unreconcilable, an emergency clinician unable to retrieve a suicidal patient's prior risk assessment, a clinic unable to reach patients). It proposes five priorities for the profession — standards (telepsychiatry, AI oversight, consent, cross-border practice), data protection, contingency plans (offline access, manual records, tested recovery), workforce training (AI literacy, preserving clinical skills), and local Arabic-language research — citing Jordan's Personal Data Protection Law No. 24 of 2023, and ends with a five-question "24-hour blackout test" for the audience and the line "Digitally enabled. Clinically led. Ethically governed. Operationally resilient." The audience is practising psychiatrists; nobody pays money. Here "paying" means psychiatrists' attention and the committee's willingness to adopt the agenda; the speaker's cost is preparation time and professional reputation, and the possible return is standing in the committee, an agenda the committee adopts, and visibility for the speaker's work in digital psychiatry.

## Believer

### 1. Who is in pain right now

The person: a consultant psychiatrist at a Ministry of Health facility, for example the National Center for Mental Health in Fuheis or a governorate hospital psychiatry clinic, working on Hakeem. Hakeem is the national EHR programme run by Electronic Health Solutions (EHS) since 2009 and is in use across MoH hospitals and clinics. This can be checked on the EHS/Hakeem site.

Their week:
- Sunday to Thursday outpatient lists are heavy. Jordan has very few psychiatrists per capita, and the WHO Mental Health Atlas country profile for Jordan gives the figure. A large refugee population adds to the load: UNHCR lists more than 500,000 registered Syrian refugees in Jordan. Exact per-clinic patient numbers are an assumption.
- The clinician depends on Hakeem for medication histories, lithium and clozapine monitoring, previous admissions and prior risk notes. When the system is slow or down, they fall back on the patient's memory and the family's plastic bag of pill boxes.
- In the evenings they get WhatsApp messages from patients and families. This is unregulated telepsychiatry, with no consent framework and no standard for record-keeping.
- Patients increasingly arrive having already "asked ChatGPT". OpenAI said on 27 Oct 2025 that about 0.15% of its weekly active users show explicit indicators of suicidal planning, which is more than a million people a week. Nothing tells the psychiatrist how to document, challenge or use that.
- Recent graduates and residents learn in an environment where the system shows the last diagnosis and the last plan. Automation bias, meaning copying forward instead of re-assessing, is now something they do every day.

A second person in pain: the emergency physician at 3 a.m. who has a suicidal patient in front of them and cannot pull up last month's risk assessment. That is vignette 3 in the deck, and every psychiatrist in the room has lived some version of it.

A third: the committee itself. It has no written profession-level position on telepsychiatry, AI oversight, consent or downtime. This is an assumption the speaker should confirm with the committee secretariat before delivery. The committee is answering these questions case by case, or not at all.

### 2. What they do today instead, and what it costs

- Downtime: improvisation. Paper is ad hoc, people phone colleagues, and patients recall their own regimens. Assumption: reconstructing a medication history without the record takes 20 to 40 minutes per complex patient. A bipolar or clozapine patient whose lithium level or ANC history cannot be retrieved gets either a delayed decision or a riskier one.
- Missed follow-ups when SMS or booking systems fail: nobody owns the list. Assumption: a clinic that loses its appointment system for a day loses the follow-up chain for every patient booked that day. For long-acting injectable and clozapine patients, a missed follow-up means a relapse risk measured in weeks.
- Telepsychiatry: informal WhatsApp and phone consultations, with no consent standard, no documentation standard and no cross-border rule for diaspora or Gulf-based patients. The cost is medico-legal exposure the individual psychiatrist carries alone.
- Data protection: compliance with Personal Data Protection Law No. 24 of 2023 is done by guesswork. The law was published in the Official Gazette in September 2023 and took effect in March 2024. Health data is sensitive data under it. Psychiatric notes are the most damaging category of health data to leak. The UnitedHealth Group disclosure that the Change Healthcare ransomware attack affected about 190 million people, and the June 2024 Synnovis attack that disrupted London NHS pathology, show what a breach costs. The checkable fact for Jordan is that the law exists. The open question is whether any psychiatry-specific guidance on applying it exists.
- AI: individual clinicians quietly use general chatbots to draft letters and notes, with no institutional rule on what may be pasted in. That is a live data-protection and quality risk at zero institutional visibility. How common it is in Jordan is an assumption. A quick show-of-hands poll at the lecture would turn it into data.
- Skills: there is no structured way to keep core interviewing and risk-assessment skills sharp as automation grows. Training relies on whoever happens to be on the ward.

The money cost is mostly hidden in hours, relapses and liability. Precise figures do not exist for Jordan, which is itself one of the lecture's arguments for the fifth priority, local research.

### 3. Why now

- Regulators started drawing the exact line this lecture draws: Illinois' Wellness and Oversight for Psychological Resources Act (HB 1806, signed August 2025) allows AI for administrative and supplementary support but bars it from making therapeutic decisions; Nevada (AB 406, 2025) and Utah (HB 452, 2025) passed laws on AI in mental health; the US FDA Digital Health Advisory Committee met on 6 Nov 2025 specifically on generative-AI-enabled digital mental health devices. The lecture's line matches where regulators in other countries are landing, and it lets Jordan's profession set its own rule before one is imported or imposed.
- Patients are already using AI for mental-health crises at scale (the OpenAI disclosure of October 2025), and there are high-profile lawsuits such as Raine v. OpenAI, filed August 2025.
- Ambient AI scribes went mainstream: Microsoft's Dragon Copilot was announced in March 2025. Automation bias stopped being theoretical and became part of how notes get written.
- Arabic-language AI became real: Jais (UAE), ALLaM (Saudi SDAIA), Fanar (Qatar). "Arabic-first access" raises the cultural-bias question immediately. An Arabic model trained on Gulf text is not a Jordanian clinician.
- Jordan's data law has moved from paper to practice: PDPL No. 24/2023 in force since March 2024.
- Infrastructure shocks are fresh: CrowdStrike (July 2024) hit hospitals worldwide; 2025 US air-traffic-control disruptions (FAA figures must be verified before delivery).
- The speaker has built and operated a bilingual AI training platform with natively authored Arabic (ar-JO) and English patient personas, keeps reports admin-only and states internally that competency scores are not yet validated — the "clinically led, ethically governed" stance the lecture argues for, if disclosed openly.

### 4. Best case at 18 months (around May 2028)

- The committee formally adopts the five priorities and sets up a small digital psychiatry working group, with the speaker as a member or rapporteur.
- A short position statement: (a) telepsychiatry minimums — consent, identity, documentation, emergencies, cross-border; (b) a written AI-oversight rule ("clinician decides diagnosis, risk and treatment"); (c) a psychiatry-specific note on applying PDPL No. 24/2023.
- At least one institution runs the "24-hour blackout test" as a tabletop drill, producing a one-page downtime packet: offline last-risk-assessment summary, current-medication printout for clozapine/lithium/LAI patients, manual follow-up list.
- A CME session on AI literacy and preserving clinical skills, using simulated-patient practice for risk assessment.
- One Arabic-language local study registered or underway, building on existing Arabic PHQ-9 validations.
- The audience's answers to the blackout questions become the working group's baseline.

What has to be true: (1) the talk is accurate — FAA figures verified or removed, Jordan claims sourced, vignettes labelled illustrative; (2) the speaker declares the Vtraining interest up front; (3) the committee has a mechanism to turn a lecture into a document, or the closing ask is to create one; (4) one institutional champion agrees to run the drill — the closing slide names one small step ("one site, one tabletop, within six months") instead of five priorities; (5) the ask stays clinician-owned standards and drills, not procurement.

## Skeptic

**Short verdict:** The talk itself will go fine. The goal behind it, a committee that adopts and acts on a five-point agenda, will very likely fail. The talk asks a professional committee to own levers it does not hold, says nothing free global guidance has not already said better, and the speaker's own product is a textbook case of the dependency risk the talk warns about.

### 1. Who will not pay (attention or adoption)
- **Overloaded MoH consultant:** "My problem is too little digital, not too much. Hakeem is slow, half my private patients are on paper." Hakeem covers ~424–482 facilities (sourced: https://www.petra.gov.jo/en/news/hakeem-system-expands-to-cover-482-healthcare-facilities-so-far-ceo; https://epinews.emphnet.net/en/news/jordans-hakeem-program-sets-regional-benchmark-in-digital-healthcare-transformation). Many private practices not on it (assumption). AI-scribe automation bias is a US-hospital problem, not today's Jordanian clinic (assumption).
- **Committee officers (the real buyer):** "Downtime is EHS's job, cybersecurity is NCSC's, the data law is MoDEE's, telemedicine rules are MoH/Cabinet's. What do you want *us* to sign?" Draft telemedicine bylaw in the pipeline since 2023 (sourced: https://jordantimes.com/node/364303); PDPL enforcement with MoDEE (sourced: https://www.modee.gov.jo/ebv4.0/root_storage/en/eb_list_page/dpo_standards.pdf); national AI ethics charter and AI strategy exist (sourced: https://iapp.org/news/a/jordan-creates-national-ethics-charter-for-ai; https://dig.watch/?p=214223); NCSC–EHS health-data cooperation announced (sourced: Petra headline, page not openable from this session). A committee will "adopt" all five at no cost; nothing changes.
- **Senior psychiatrists:** "Priority 4 — is the remedy your simulation platform?" The Believer's best case puts the speaker's product inside the agenda; disclosure makes the conflict visible, not absent.
- **Academics:** "This is WHO guidance read aloud. Where is the Jordanian evidence?"

### 2. Competitors
- WHO, *Ethics and governance of AI for health: LMMs* (Jan 2024, 40+ recommendations) (sourced: https://www.who.int/news/item/18-01-2024-who-releases-ai-ethics-and-governance-guidance-for-large-multi-modal-models).
- APA position statements: *Role of Augmented Intelligence* (Mar 2024) and *Use of AI for Mental Health Services and Treatment* (2026) (sourced: psychiatry.org).
- WPA Telepsychiatry Global Guidelines (2021, LMIC-oriented) (sourced: https://wpanet.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/FINAL_2021_WPA_Telepsychiatry-Guidelines-23Sep202111001.pdf); WPA Working Group on Digitalisation 2023–2026 plan.
- ONC SAFER Guide, Contingency Planning — a free, more mature blackout test (sourced: https://www.healthit.gov/safer/).
- Jordanian incumbents: MoDEE (AI policy 2020, strategy 2023–2027, ethics charter, PDPL directorate; PDPL took effect 17 Mar 2024 and the compliance window closed 16 Mar 2025 (sourced: https://www.clym.io/regulations/law-no-24-of-2023-jordan) — the Believer's "inside the compliance window" is out of date); MoH–MoDEE Digital Health Centre telemedicine to 12 public hospitals (sourced: Jordan Times); Jordan Medical Association position on telemedicine; a Mar 2026 Jordanian AI-in-health policy paper (ZUJ); Jordanian Association of Psychiatrists biennial congress.
- Manual incumbent: pill-box bag, family memory, paper downtime forms, clinic WhatsApp group. The one Hakeem incident found was a ~90-minute delay, not a shutdown (sourced: https://www.jordannews.jo/Section-109/News/Salt-hospital-director-addresses-public-concerns-6283).

### 3. What the speaker is too close to see
The hidden assumption is that the speaker stands outside the risk he describes. His platform runs on US infrastructure (Supabase us-east-1, Vercel iad1 — sourced: repo docs), depends on single US AI vendors (OpenAI / Vercel AI Gateway, ElevenLabs), and uses an LLM examiner whose scores the project calls "not yet validated" (CLAUDE.md). One floor question exposes it: "You say AI must not make judgment calls and data must stay governed; your platform sends Jordanian trainee transcripts to US servers and has GPT grade their skill with an unvalidated score." Whether PDPL cross-border rules apply to trainee data is open (assumption). Fix: keep the product off stage, or present it as a case study of its own dependency risks. Second hidden assumption: the danger is over-dependence on digital; for most of the audience it is under-digitisation (assumption).

### 4. Most likely death in year one
Polite applause, "the committee thanks Dr. Zayed", no owner, no document, no drill. ~40 items across 12 slides in 15 minutes (~75 s/slide, arithmetic); nothing names a person, a date or a decision; every priority needs a body not in the room. Second-most-likely: a credibility hit from the slides (ATC slide still marked preliminary, US "why now" items 12–15 months stale, wrong PDPL compliance-window claim).

### 5. Where the Believer's case is weakest
1. "Who is in pain" is almost all assumption; the one hard local fact (~90-min Hakeem delay) is smaller than the hypothetical.
2. Absence of evidence used as evidence of need — circular.
3. "Why now" is imported and stale; nothing on what changed in Jordan in 2026.
4. The Believer's own conditions rewrite the talk, and the champion is never named.
5. "Committee adopts five priorities" is too easy to meet; a real test is one signed artefact — a psychiatric downtime minimum dataset (last risk assessment, clozapine/lithium/LAI list, emergency contact) psychiatrists can own and print without EHS/NCSC/MoDEE.
6. Upside listed, vendor-lobbying reputational downside ignored.

**Survives?** As a 15-minute talk, yes. As an agenda vehicle, not in this form — unless cut to one psychiatrist-owned deliverable plus one named pilot site, stale/unverified US material removed, and the platform kept off stage or presented as a case study of its own risks.

## Investor

**No cash shows up from this talk. The only things it can earn are a committee decision and named commitments. If the talk is used to sell Vtraining, it loses both.** "Customer" = a psychiatrist or institution that commits to a specific, checkable action.

- Pilot site: a half-day tabletop, ~4 h × ~6 staff (assumption).

## Judge

VERDICT: FIX FIRST

WHY: The Skeptic made the strongest argument and was right about what goes wrong: the talk asks a committee to own five priorities that belong to EHS, NCSC, MoDEE and MoH, and the speaker's own platform does what the talk warns against (US servers, single US AI vendors, an LLM giving scores the project itself calls "not yet validated"); the Believer's best case does not survive that. The Believer made the weakest argument, because almost all the pain is assumed and the "why now" is imported, 12–15 months old, and partly wrong (the PDPL compliance window closed on 16 Mar 2025, so the profession is not "inside" it); the Believer's own preconditions (verify or remove FAA, disclose up front, a named champion, one small ask) admit that the current deck cannot win the agenda. The Investor settled it: committee adoption costs the committee nothing, so the only real signal is named psychiatrists and a chair committing to one checkable artefact before the talk; the 15-minute lecture is worth giving, but the agenda in its current form is a polite-applause machine.

BIGGEST RISK: The talk ends with "the committee thanks Dr. Zayed" and nothing else: no owner, no document, no drill, because every priority needs a body not in the room and nothing on the slides names a person, a date or a decision.

DE-RISK IT: Today, send the committee chair this message: "Will you put a vote on adopting a one-page psychiatric downtime sheet (last risk assessment, clozapine/lithium/LAI list, emergency contact) on the next meeting agenda, with a date?" A dated yes means the agenda has a mechanism. "Send it and we'll see", or no date, means you give a plain lecture and drop the agenda.

IF FIX FIRST: Replace the five priorities with one psychiatrist-owned deliverable, the downtime minimum-dataset sheet, plus pilot sites that agreed in advance and are named on slide 12; delete the unverified FAA figures, the stale US items and the wrong PDPL claim; and cut Vtraining to a single disclosure line, or present it openly as a case study of its own dependency risks.
